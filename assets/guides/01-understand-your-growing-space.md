# Understand your growing space

Before choosing a plant, get to know the place where it will grow. Light, drainage, water access, and exposure help determine which plants are a good match.

## What you will learn
- Observe light rather than guessing from a room's brightness.
- Distinguish container, raised-bed, and in-ground growing.
- Identify drainage and exposure concerns before planting.

## 1. Choose one growing location
Start with a specific place: a patio corner, garden bed, or window shelf. Record whether it is indoors, outdoors, or protected by a greenhouse. A greenhouse is not automatically frost-free.

## 2. Observe the light
Check the location in the morning, around midday, and in the afternoon. Estimate how long direct sun reaches it and note shade from buildings or trees. Repeat observations when seasons change.

Outdoor full-sun crops often need at least six hours of direct sun; follow the selected plant's guidance. Indoor bright indirect light is not interchangeable with outdoor full sun. A room can look bright to a person while offering too little light for seedlings. Consider supplemental lighting when starting seeds indoors. [S1][S2]

If you have not observed the location yet, select “Not sure” rather than guessing.

## 3. Check the growing method and drainage
For containers, select a pot with drainage openings and an appropriate growing mix. A decorative outer pot can hold an inner nursery pot, but let the inner pot drain and empty collected water rather than leaving roots standing in it. [S3]

For garden beds, observe whether water pools after rain or irrigation. Soil texture affects moisture retention and drainage; choose plants and growing methods that suit the site. [S4]

## 4. Notice exposure and practical access
Record strong wind, reflected heat, shelter, and whether water is easy to reach. These observations are context, not an automatic watering formula. Check the actual growing medium before following a care action.
