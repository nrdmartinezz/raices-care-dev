# Start seeds

This beginner guide focuses on starting suitable garden seeds indoors. It is not a universal protocol for tree seeds, specialized succulent propagation, or seeds needing treatments such as cold stratification. Follow the packet and reviewed species guidance for those cases.

## What you will learn
- Decide whether indoor starting is appropriate.
- Prepare containers, growing medium, and labels.
- Sow at the correct depth and monitor moisture and light.

## Before you begin
Confirm the seed's identity, planting method, and local timing. Starting too early can leave you managing plants indoors longer than expected. Do not use a hardiness zone alone to choose a sowing date; keep dates unset when regional timing has not been reviewed.

## Supplies
- Seeds with readable instructions.
- Clean containers or cells with drainage holes.
- Seed-starting mix appropriate for the seed.
- A tray to catch water.
- Labels and a marker.
- A gentle watering method.
- Suitable lighting and a timer if needed.
- An optional heat mat when the seed requires warmer germination conditions. [S1][S2]

## Step 1. Prepare and label
Moisten the seed-starting mix before filling containers. Label the plant, variety, and sowing date. Keep drainage openings clear. Individual cells make later handling easier than a crowded shared container. [S1]

## Step 2. Sow according to the packet
Follow the stated depth and any light/darkness requirements. Do not apply a universal burial depth. If special preparation is needed and you do not have instructions, pause and find species-specific guidance. [S1][S3]

## Step 3. Manage moisture and warmth
Keep the medium moist enough for germination without leaving it waterlogged. Check it instead of watering by a fixed daily amount. Use the seed's temperature guidance; do not give every seed the same heat setting. If using a humidity cover, monitor it and remove the cover after germination. [S1][S3]

## Step 4. Provide seedling light
Once seedlings emerge, provide adequate light. A windowsill may not supply enough consistent light. Minnesota Extension recommends 12–16 hours of light daily for indoor seedlings; this is general seedling guidance, not a requirement for every mature houseplant. Follow fixture instructions for distance and monitor for heat or stretching. Leave a dark period each day. [S1][S4]

## Step 5. Observe growth
Record the first visible sprout and later true leaves. If seedlings crowd one another, follow crop-specific thinning instructions. Do not pull tangled roots apart casually. [S1]

## Step 6. Plan the next move
Some seedlings need a larger pot before outdoor planting. Do not move them outdoors just because a fixed number of days has elapsed. Review plant readiness, local conditions, and hardening-off instructions before outdoor transplanting. [S1][S3]

## If something goes wrong
- No sprouts: recheck the expected germination range, depth, moisture, and temperature instructions.
- Long, weak growth: review lighting and crowding rather than assuming fertilizer is needed.
- Persistently wet mix: check drainage and watering practices.
- Collapsing seedlings: keep a record and seek reviewed troubleshooting guidance; do not automatically recommend a chemical treatment. [S1]

## Quick check
Question: Should every seed be covered with the same amount of mix?
Answer: No. Depth and light requirements vary; follow the seed-specific instructions. [S1]
